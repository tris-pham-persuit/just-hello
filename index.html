just hello

please do say just hello in chat

Imagine waving at a colleague in the corridor, and they hand you a form to fill in before you can say hello! 🤦‍♀️

❌ Don't do this

Note that Keith only wanted to say hi. Tim answered with a link, and now neither of them is having a nice afternoon. In fact, Keith may never message Tim again, and the question he did not have yet will never be asked.

People who do this are generally trying to be efficient, and that's great! But it's 2026, and half of what lands in your inbox was written by a machine. A plain hi is one of the few messages left that a person typed on purpose. So despite best intentions, you're actually just making a human feel like a support ticket (and kinda unfriendly).

The same goes for:

  • "tl;dr?"
  • "Hi. Please state your question."
  • "Is there a point to this?"
  • etc.

Just say hello back! 🤗

✅ Instead try this

If it feels a bit slow to say hello and wait, you can still add your question a minute later. Hello first, question when the other person has answered. Or write your question in advance, and paste it when the moment feels right. Both work.

For example:

  • "hey, how's your day going? I have a question when you have a minute"
  • "Hi! No rush at all, I'll be here"
  • "hello :) I've written down what I need, ready when you are"
  • etc.

It may seem trivial, but starting with hello means the chat is a conversation, not only a transaction. If the other person is away, the hello can simply wait for them.

When done right, everybody feels a little less like a ticket queue. 🎉

👋 Why just hello?

  1. Lately this page is back in everyone's feed.

    Maybe it's because AI answers instantly now, and we expect people to do the same. But people are not chatbots. Sometimes we just want to say hi.

  2. Do you always reply right away?

    Or do you sometimes stop and think? Somebody is waiting for your answer right now, and nobody wrote a manifesto about it. Waiting is fine, in both directions.

  3. Chat is async, so nobody has to stare at "hi".

    If Tim is waiting for the next message, he can go and do something else. That's what async means. And if Tim had nobody to talk to today, maybe that hi was the best message he got.

  4. A typed hi is a small sign that a human is here.

    Many messages are now written by an AI and read by an AI summary. A plain hi from someone who is really there is the one message nobody bothered to automate.

  5. People have all sorts of ways to talk.

    Some are shy, some are awkward, some need a moment to warm up. People are not robots, and a website that tells them how to talk to you is a good reason for them to stop asking you anything. (One Hacker News commenter said it better.) Yes, this page is a manifesto too. We know.

  6. We already wait for slow things.

    We wait for an AI that types one word at a time, and we shrug when the API returns an error. A colleague who takes four minutes to get to the point can have the same grace.

  7. Machines never forget to reply. People do.

    When that happens, be kind about it. That is how you know they are human.

This is only half serious (kinda 👀), so please don't take it out on the person who sent you here.

That said, if you see this site's URL in someone's status or bio, be ready for a warm hello, and possibly a long chat.
